Notes on coffee, people & the rituals

The Many Ways
We Drink Coffee.

We went looking for coffee habits. What we found were little rituals, shortcuts, obsessions, routines, rewards and a surprising number of very personal definitions of what counts as a “good cup.”

Thanks to the people who shared theirs
01 — THE QUESTION

What is coffee actually doing for you?

Not what should it do. Not what coffee people on the internet say it does. What happens in the few minutes before someone makes a cup, buys one, or decides they absolutely need one?

There isn't one way to be a coffee person.

For some, coffee is the first switch of the day. For some, it is fuel before a run. For some, the brewing process deserves scales, temperatures and four carefully timed pours. For others, coffee is an iced latte, a café, a conversation or simply the fastest available route to being awake.

And here are some honourable mentions.

02 — FIVE WAYS IN

Five kinds of coffee moments.

These aren't boxes. Most people move between them depending on the day. Think of them as different jobs a cup can have.

The Starter

“The day begins when the coffee does.”

Coffee is part of the architecture of the morning. The exact cup matters because the ritual matters.

The Performer

“Give me caffeine. I have things to do.”

Coffee is a functional button: wake up, work, run, gym. The cup does not need a ceremony to do its job.

The Explorer

“What happens if I change the grind?”

Beans, extraction, processing and equipment are part of the fun. Sometimes the coffee is the hobby.

The Bridge

“I like good coffee. I just don’t always have 15 minutes.”

A middle ground: people whose taste has moved ahead of their willingness or ability to brew every time.

The Indulger

“Let’s get a cold coffee.”

Coffee is pleasure, atmosphere, refreshment, a treat, or a reason to go somewhere. Sometimes all of those at once.

03 — THE STARTERS

Sattu & Chaitanya

For some people, coffee is not an optional part of the morning. It is the thing that makes the morning feel assembled.

Sattu
The Starter

Thirty to forty minutes after waking, coffee arrives.

Sattu waits before his first cup, gets ready for the day, and then reaches for Davidoff 57. He calls it an “amazing kickstart.” He knows exactly how he likes it: bitter enough, chocolatey, smooth.

Chaitanya
The Starter

For Chaitanya, coffee is simply part of the morning.

His usual is black Nescafé, recently upgraded to the Roastery version. The ritual itself, however, remains beautifully uncomplicated: boil water, add coffee, stir, done.

04 — THE PERFORMERS

Shivam, Pranjul & Rishab

Different coffee preferences. Similar underlying principle: sometimes coffee’s most important job is to get you somewhere.

Shivam
The Performer

Wake up. Coffee. Gym.

Shivam’s morning coffee is almost comically efficient. Coffee costs around ₹10, takes essentially no time, and exists to prepare him for the workout. He likes the “perfect bitter” taste and has little interest in making the occasion more complicated.

A coffee routine with the efficiency of a push notification.

Pranjul
The Performer

Caffeine first. Brewing method second.

Pranjul reaches for coffee when the caffeine gap starts to become noticeable often immediately after waking. At home, the moka pot gives him the stronger cup he prefers. When travelling, instant coffee is everywhere, so the compromise is easy.

The moka pot is the preference. Instant is the contingency plan.

Rishab
The Performer / The Explorer

He makes 18-hour cold brew. He also has an emergency mode.

Rishab prepares Araku cold brew in a 1:10–1:12 ratio for 18 hours, in a big batch. But when his own brew is not available, an iced Americano becomes a tool: less-defined taste, but the caffeine still does its job.

The same person can be a coffee romantic at home and a caffeine realist outside.

05 — THE EXPLORERS

Ankur, Kakkar & Sameeha

For these three, coffee becomes something to learn, tune, compare and sometimes just enjoy very, very slowly.

Ankur
The Explorer

Weekdays have coffee. Weekends have coffee events.

V60, 18 grams, measured temperature, four pours, timing, rest. Ankur’s weekday coffee is streamlined; weekends are allowed to stretch out. Washed coffees make sense when the week is moving. Naturals belong to slower days, when there is time to talk and enjoy the process.

Some people have a weekend. Ankur has a weekend coffee protocol.

Kakkar
The Explorer

Curious, but not trying to become a coffee sommelier.

Kakkar uses a moka pot and grinder, pays attention to temperature and avoids burning the coffee. He knows specialty coffee can taste different, and wants to understand washed versus natural versus fermented but has no desire to start identifying “cheesecake notes”.

A healthy reminder that you can care about coffee without making coffee your entire personality.

Sameeha
The Explorer

Espresso in the morning. Guava cold brew when the mood strikes.

Sameeha uses an espresso machine, WDT and extraction time to get her morning shots right. Then there is the other side of coffee: flavoured cold brews, maple, sea salt, vanilla, caramel.

The espresso machine says precision. The guava cold brew says: let’s have some fun.

06 — THE BRIDGE

Arnav & Rishita

Perhaps the most interesting place to be: wanting better coffee without wanting every part of the work that can come with it.

Arnav
The Bridge / The Explorer

V60 in the morning. Bru after a nap.

Arnav owns an AeroPress, espresso machine and V60 setup. He can carefully weigh 18g of beans and control his pour. But if he needs coffee quickly, instant wins because the V60 takes time. He keeps instant coffee at home for exactly these moments.

Having three coffee machines does not make you immune to wanting coffee right now.

Rishita
The Bridge / The Indulger

Davidoff at home. Blue Tokai iced latte at the office.

At home, Rishita dissolves Davidoff in a little warm water, gets it frothy, pours it over ice and adds milk. At the office, the ritual becomes a Blue Tokai iced latte. She wants more high-quality options she can make at home without turning coffee into a project.

The café version is the experience. The home version is the convenience. She wants the gap between them to shrink.

07 — THE INDULGERS

Moushami & Swatik

Here coffee behaves less like a brewing discipline and more like a drink worth going out for.

Moushami
The Indulger

Sometimes coffee is simply the thing you feel like having.

Moushami's coffee doesn't need to wake her up, improve her focus, or begin a carefully constructed morning ritual. The particular cup might be too milky, too sweet, or not quite coffee forward enoughand she'll notice. But coffee can still belong in the same world as the other little refreshments we reach for during the day: something cold, comforting, and enjoyable.

Coffee doesn't always have to do something. Sometimes it just has to feel good.

Swatik
The Indulger / The Performer

₹2 before the run. ₹340 for the café version.

Swatik’s home coffee is a ₹2 Nescafé sachet before a 5km run. Fast, portable, effective. But coffee at a café is something else: hazelnut frappe, extra espresso, aroma, taste and vibe. Coffee is also the drink he reaches for when he wants to sit somewhere.

The pre-workout coffee and the café coffee may share a name. They are doing completely different jobs.

08 — THINGS WE LEARNED

A few coffee truths.

Not laws. Not statistics. Just patterns that kept appearing in people’s stories.

Good coffee is contextual. A person can care deeply about taste at home and care about nothing except caffeine when they are late.
Convenience is not the opposite of quality. Several people want better coffee precisely because they do not want brewing equipment, complicated preparation or extra time.
The ritual can be the product. For some, weighing beans and pouring water is not work around the coffee. It is part of the reason for the coffee.
“Coffee” can mean wildly different drinks. Black instant, V60, espresso, moka pot, cold brew, iced latte and hazelnut frappe all appeared in these stories.
A bad coffee does not always get abandoned. Sometimes you drink it because you paid for it. Sometimes because you need the caffeine. Sometimes because, well, it is already in your hand.
People know more than they think. Some people talk in extraction times and processing methods. Others simply know exactly what tastes right to them. Both are useful kinds of knowledge.
09 — THANK YOU

Behind every cup is a person trying to make a moment work.

Thank you for letting us into one of yours. Whether coffee is your ritual, your fuel, your hobby, your treat, your excuse to meet someone or just something you occasionally drink we think the little details are worth paying attention to.

And yes, we noticed the difference between ₹2 coffee and ₹900 coffee. We also noticed that both can be exactly right.
